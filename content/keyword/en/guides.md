# Keyword substitution: guides

## Guide 1 — your first keyword cipher

1. Open the tool and type the keyword `SHADOW`. Watch the alphabet
   strip: the bottom row starts `S H A D O W`, then continues
   `B C E F G…` — the keyword letters first, everything else in order.
2. Type `MEET ME AT NOON`. Notice how every E encrypts to the same
   letter — that sameness is what later breaks the cipher.
3. Turn on "Keep spaces" and encrypt again. The word lengths now travel
   with the message — convenient for the reader, and a gift for the
   codebreaker.

## Guide 2 — the two alphabets

1. Encrypt the same message with `KRYPTOS`, once with "Continue after
   the keyword" off and once on. Compare the strips: the first half is
   identical, the second half rotates.
2. Both alphabets are legitimate keyword alphabets — which is why a
   codebreaker who recovers an alphabet still has to check whether it
   spells a keyword at all.
3. Switch to decrypt mode and paste your own cryptogram. Encryption and
   decryption use the same two rows in opposite directions.

## Guide 3 — from keyword to free alphabet

1. Tick "Free alphabet" and type any 26 letters in a scrambled order —
   the tool validates that every letter appears exactly once.
2. Encrypt a message and study the strip: nothing marks the alphabet as
   keyword-built, because it no longer is.
3. This is the honest picture of every substitution cipher: one fixed,
   arbitrary mapping. The keyword alphabet is a special, well-behaved
   case of it — and special cases leak their specialness.
