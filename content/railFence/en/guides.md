# Rail fence: guides

## Guide 1 — your first zigzag

1. Open the tool, leave the rails at 3 and type `MEET ME AT NOON`.
   Watch the {{term:zigzag}} draw itself: the first letters fall down
   the diagonal, then bounce.
2. Compare the ciphertext with the fence: it is the rails concatenated,
   top to bottom — nothing else.
3. Switch to decrypt and paste the cryptogram back. The same fence
   rebuilds the message.

## Guide 2 — the offset

1. Set the offset to 1 (start on the middle rail) and encrypt the same
   message again.
2. Compare the two cryptograms: the offset version no longer starts
   with the message's first letter — the classic fence's most obvious
   tell is gone.
3. Try the maximum offset (start on the bottom rail): the zigzag runs
   upward first. Every setting is just a different way of walking the
   same fence.

## Guide 3 — from rail fence to columnar

1. Encrypt a message on 3 rails and look closely at the rails: each one
   is a "column" of a zigzag-shaped grid.
2. Now open the Columnar tool, encrypt the same message with a 3-letter
   keyword, and compare: the columnar grid is the same idea with
   straight columns instead of a diagonal.
3. That is why both ciphers fall to the same weapon — rebuilding the
   order — and why the columnar cracker's step two is the rail fence
   slider's big brother.
